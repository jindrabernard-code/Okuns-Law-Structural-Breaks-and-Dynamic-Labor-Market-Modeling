# Econometric Analysis of Okun's Law: Structural Breaks and Dynamic Labor Market Modeling

> Applied Econometrics Project — Masaryk University, 2026 | Apr 2026 – May 2026
>
> ## Overview
>
> This project analyzes the empirical validity of Okun's Law using quarterly U.S. macroeconomic data from 1955–2025, combining classical econometric modeling with structural break detection and dynamic time-series analysis. The research examines how labor market responsiveness to output fluctuations has changed across different economic regimes.
>
> ## Data
>
> - Source: Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis (FRED)
> - - Coverage: Quarterly U.S. data, 1955–2025
>   - - Variables: Real GDP, unemployment rate
>     - - Processing: Cyclical component extraction via detrending
>      
>       - ## Methodology
>      
>       - ### Core Models
>       - - Static Okun specification (OLS)
>         - - Dynamic Okun specification (distributed lag models)
> - Long-run coefficient estimation
>
> - ### Advanced Techniques
> - - Structural break detection: Bai–Perron and Chow tests
>   - - Asymmetric business cycle modeling (expansion vs. recession)
>     - - Dynamic distributed lag models
>       - - Uncertainty quantification: Delta method and Monte Carlo simulation
>        
>         - ## Key Findings
>        
>         - - Statistically significant structural break identified after the **2008 financial crisis**
>           - - Labor market responsiveness to GDP fluctuations **changed materially** across economic regimes
>             - - Asymmetric effects: unemployment responds differently during expansions vs. recessions
>               - - Long-run Okun coefficient stable in pre-2008 period, weakened post-crisis
>
